## Why is the sky blue?

Sunlight looks white, but it actually contains many different wavelengths of [[anh-sang-kha-kien|visible light]].

When light enters the atmosphere, it interacts with gas molecules, mainly nitrogen and oxygen.

These molecules cause **Rayleigh scattering**, in which short-wavelength light is scattered more strongly than long-wavelength light. So blue light is spread across the sky much more strongly than red light. 

That is why, when we look up at the sky, blue light reaches our eyes from almost every direction.

### But why isn't the sky violet?

Violet light has an even shorter wavelength and is scattered very strongly.

However, the human eye is less sensitive to violet than to blue, and the spectrum of sunlight reaching the eye and absorption in the atmosphere also contribute to making the overall colour we perceive mainly **blue**. 

## Why are sunsets red?

When the Sun is near the horizon, its light has to travel a longer path through the atmosphere.

Along the way:

- Most of the blue and violet light has been scattered out of the direct line of sight.
- Longer wavelengths such as yellow, orange and red remain in greater amounts on the path to the eye.

As a result, the Sun and the sky around the horizon turn orange or red. Dust and aerosols in the atmosphere can change sunset colours even more. 

## Why are clouds white?

Clouds are made of countless **tiny water droplets and ice crystals** suspended in the atmosphere.

These particles are much larger than the gas molecules that cause Rayleigh scattering. They scatter the many wavelengths of visible light fairly evenly.

When the colours are scattered together and reach our eyes, the cloud looks **white**. 

## Why are rain clouds grey or black?

Thick clouds do not actually turn into grey material.

When a cloud is thick enough, sunlight has to undergo many rounds of scattering, and some of the light is absorbed or redirected elsewhere.

So less light escapes through the base of the cloud.

Seen from the ground, the underside of the cloud becomes:

**White → Grey → Dark grey**

A very thick thundercloud can therefore look almost black, even though the water droplets inside are not black at all.

## Does a cloud really weigh hundreds of tonnes?

Yes.

Clouds look as light as cotton wool, but they hold a very large amount of water spread over an enormous volume.

What keeps them from falling as a mass of water is that the droplets inside are tiny. They fall very slowly and are held up by air currents.

When droplets or ice crystals collide, merge and become large enough, gravity overcomes the air currents holding them up.

They then fall as:

- Rain.
- Snow.
- Hail.

## The night sky is a window into the past

When we look up at the stars at night, we do not see them as they are at this very moment.

The reason is simple:

**Light takes time to travel.**

Light from the Sun takes about 8 minutes to reach Earth. So we always see the Sun as it was about 8 minutes ago. 

The nearest star to the Solar System is more than 4 [[nam-anh-sang|light-years]] away, so the light we see today left that star more than 4 years ago. 

And the Andromeda galaxy is about **2.5 million light-years** away.

That means:

**We are seeing Andromeda as it was about 2.5 million years ago.** 

For galaxies billions of light-years farther away, telescopes are really observing very early stages in the history of the universe. This is why telescopes are sometimes likened to **"time machines"**. 

## Conclusion

🌤️ The sky is blue because gas molecules scatter short-wavelength light more strongly.

🌅 Sunsets are red because the light has to pass through more atmosphere, so most of the blue light is scattered out of the line of sight.

☁️ Clouds are white because water droplets and ice crystals scatter many colours of light at once.

🌌 And when night falls, the sky becomes a window looking back in time, where each [[photon]] carries the image of an object at the moment it left its source.

**So looking up at the sky is not just looking out into space. In a very real sense, we are also looking into the universe's past.**
