## 🧬 What does evolution actually care about?

Evolution has no purpose, emotions or morality.

It is simply the process by which traits that help organisms survive and reproduce effectively are more likely to be passed on.

From this point of view:

> A successful [[gen|gene]] is not one that helps the body live forever, but one that helps it leave many copies of itself.

If a trait gives an advantage during the reproductive years, it can be maintained over many generations even if it is harmful in old age.

---

## ⏳ Why does the body age?

Evolutionary biologists have put forward several explanations for ageing.

### Accumulation of late-acting mutations

The biologist Peter Medawar proposed that harmful [[dot-bien|mutations]] that appear in old age are usually poorly weeded out by natural selection.

The reasons are:

- Many individuals in the wild do not live long enough for those mutations to show their effects.
- If a mutation only causes harm after reproductive age, selection pressure on it is very weak.

Over time, unfavourable mutations that act in old age can accumulate in a population.

### Antagonistic pleiotropy

The biologist George C. Williams proposed that some genes may:

- Be beneficial when young.
- Be harmful in old age.

For example:

- A mechanism that drives rapid cell growth may help the body be strong in youth.
- But after many decades, the same mechanism may raise the risk of cancer.

If the benefit during the reproductive years outweighs the later harm, evolution will still keep that gene.

## 🧓 The body was not designed to last forever

A car needs constant maintenance to keep running for a long time.

So does a living body.

However, repairing and maintaining the body costs a great deal of energy.

According to some evolutionary theories, nature has to keep "balancing the budget":

- Investment in growth.
- Investment in reproduction.
- Investment in body maintenance.

All three cannot be maximised at once.

When resources are limited, life usually prioritises:

> Reproduction first, long-term maintenance later.

That is one of the reasons ageing appears in almost all multicellular organisms.

---

## 🌱 What would happen without death?

At first, a world of immortals sounds ideal.

But from an ecological and evolutionary point of view, it could create serious problems.

### Resources would quickly run out

Every organism needs:

- Food.
- Living space.
- Water.
- Energy.

If no individual ever died, the number of organisms would keep growing until the ecosystem collapsed.

### Evolution would slow down

Evolution depends on:

- Reproduction.
- Genetic variation.
- The replacement of generations.

If the older generation lived forever, new variants would have little chance of spreading through the population.

### The ability to adapt would decline

The environment is always changing.

New generations are life's chance to try out new combinations of genes better suited to current conditions.

The death of individuals creates room for that process.

---

## 🦖 No species is the "final destination"

Humans often regard themselves as the pinnacle of evolution.

But evolution has no destination.

Over more than 4 billion years of the history of life:

> More than 99% of all species that have ever existed have gone extinct.

Dinosaurs dominated Earth for more than 160 million years.

Today they no longer play that role.

From a biological point of view, humans are not guaranteed any special place in the distant future either.

We are just one branch on life's vast evolutionary tree.

---

## 🧠 What makes humans different

Interestingly, humans are not just a product of evolution.

Humans are also able to reflect on evolution.

We know that:

- We will grow old.
- We will die.
- Life is finite.

This awareness brings a great deal of anxiety, but it also creates very special things:

- Art.
- Philosophy.
- Science.
- Literature.
- Love.
- Empathy.

These do not directly serve the passing on of genes, but they help create the meaning of human life.

---

## 🌌 Death and the meaning of life

From the universe's point of view, the atoms in your body do not really disappear when you die.

They will:

- Return to the environment.
- Take part in other chemical cycles.
- Become part of new forms of life.

The atoms that make up our bodies today once belonged to:

- Ancient stars.
- Primordial rock and soil.
- Organisms that lived long before humans appeared.

In a material sense:

> We are only borrowing the universe's atoms for a short while.

---

## 📌 Conclusion

Evolutionary biology shows that death is not an unexpected mistake of nature. It is a consequence of the way natural selection has operated over billions of years.

🧬 Evolution prioritises passing on genes over the immortality of the individual.

🌱 Death makes room for new generations and keeps life able to adapt.

🌍 No species is guaranteed to exist forever.

However, humans have something that evolution cannot fully determine:

> The ability to create meaning for their own lives.

Evolution may care only about passing on genes, but art, friendship, knowledge, love and discovery let us live for many purposes greater than that.
