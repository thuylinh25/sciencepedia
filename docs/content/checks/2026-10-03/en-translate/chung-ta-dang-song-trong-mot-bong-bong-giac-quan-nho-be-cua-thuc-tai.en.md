## 👁️ The limits of human senses

Our senses were shaped to serve survival, not to observe the whole universe.

### Hearing

The human ear can hear only sounds in the range of:

> **20 Hz to about 20,000 Hz**

Beyond this range there are countless sounds that we cannot perceive at all.

For example:

- Elephants use infrasound to communicate over very long distances.
- Bats and dolphins use ultrasound to locate things around them.

A large part of the "world of sound" exists, yet humans are entirely unaware of it.

### Vision

The human eye can see only light with wavelengths of about:

> **380 to 700 nanometres**

This is just a tiny slice of the whole [[buc-xa-dien-tu|electromagnetic spectrum]].

Beyond this band there are also:

- Infrared.
- Ultraviolet.
- Radio waves.
- Microwaves.
- X-rays.
- Gamma rays.

Your body is passed through by these forms of radiation every day, but the human eye cannot see them at all.

## 🌎 Each species lives in a different "reality"

The biologist Jakob von Uexküll called each organism's own perceived world its:

> **Umwelt**

You can think of it as the "subjective universe" that each species experiences.

### The world of snakes

Some snakes have organs that sense infrared.

They see not only the shape of their prey but also:

- Body heat.
- Warm and cold areas in their surroundings.

### The world of bees

Many flowers have ultraviolet patterns that are invisible to the human eye.

To bees:

- These flowers glow like giant signposts.
- They point the way to the nectar.

### The world of dogs

Dogs detect a great many smells that humans miss — although the often-quoted figure of "tens of thousands of times more powerful" is not backed by any common measurement, and for some odours humans are actually more sensitive than dogs.

If humans "read" the world mainly through sight, dogs do so largely through smell.

An ordinary park to us may be a vast "library of information" to a dog.

## 🧠 The brain does not see reality directly

Surprisingly, the eyes do not really "see".

The ears do not really "hear".

The senses only collect signals.

The brain then:

- Filters out most of the data.
- Reconstructs it into images.
- Interprets it as sounds.
- Assigns meaning to things.

In other words:

> What we experience is not reality itself but a model of reality built by the brain.

Colour, sound and the sensation of hot and cold are all the brain's ways of interpreting information from the environment.

## ❓ How much are we missing?

This is one of the most intriguing questions in science and philosophy.

We know for certain that:

- There are forms of radiation the eye cannot see.
- There are sounds the ear cannot hear.
- There are chemical signals the nose cannot smell.
- There are physical phenomena that can only be detected with instruments.

So the question is not:

> "Are we missing anything?"

But rather:

> "How much are we missing?"

There may still be aspects of nature that human senses were simply not built to detect.

## 🔬 Science is a way of enlarging the sensory bubble

Remarkably, humans have invented tools to go beyond their biological limits.

Telescopes let us see:

- Radio waves.
- Infrared.
- X-rays.

Microscopes let us see:

- Cells.
- Bacteria.
- Viruses.

Particle detectors let us detect:

- Neutrinos.
- Cosmic rays.
- Elementary particles.

In a sense:

> Science is the ongoing process of enlarging humanity's sensory bubble.

## 📌 Conclusion

🌌 Every organism lives in a "reality" created by its own senses. Humans are no exception.

What we see is only a very small part of the physical world that exists around us. Science does not prove that the world is an illusion, but it does show that our perception is only a limited slice of a far larger reality.

Perhaps the most important lesson is humility:

> What we know is always much smaller than what still lies hidden beyond the edge of our perception.
