## What is a proton?

A proton is a positively charged particle found in the atomic nucleus.

The number of protons determines which element an atom belongs to:

- Hydrogen has 1 proton.
- Carbon has 6 protons.
- Oxygen has 8 protons.
- Iron has 26 protons.

Changing the number of protons means changing the element.

So the stability of the proton is fundamental to the long-term existence of ordinary matter.

## Is the proton really immortal?

According to the Standard Model of particle physics, the proton is extremely stable.

The proton is the lightest baryon, so it cannot simply decay into a lighter baryon while still conserving baryon number.

However, that does not prove that the proton lasts forever.

Some **Grand Unified Theories (GUTs)** hold that baryon number may not be absolutely conserved.

If these theories are right, the proton could decay into lighter particles.

## How long does a proton live?

Nobody knows exactly, because:

> **Proton decay has never been observed.**

Experiments such as **Super-Kamiokande** in Japan monitor an enormous volume of water containing a huge number of protons, looking for rare decay signals.

Current results show that the proton's lifetime, depending on the decay channel considered, must be greater than about:

**10³⁴ years**

For comparison:

**Age of the universe ≈ 1.38 × 10¹⁰ years**

That means the proton may last at least thousands of billions of billions of times longer than the current age of the universe.

## What would happen if protons really do decay?

If the proton is not immortal, the universe would not change suddenly.

There would be no giant explosion.

Instead, matter would disappear extremely slowly, over spans of time far beyond human imagination.

### Atoms gradually become unstable

If protons decay, atomic nuclei no longer last forever.

Over extremely long times:

- The elements disappear.
- Atomic structure breaks down.
- Ordinary matter is gradually converted into lighter particles and radiation.

### Chemistry eventually disappears too

Chemistry exists because of atoms.

If the proton is ultimately unstable, structures such as:

- Water.
- Rock.
- Metals.
- Organic molecules.

cannot exist forever.

Not because they are destroyed from outside, but because the very components that make up their nuclei are decaying.

## Even "stellar corpses" do not last forever

Long after the last stars stop shining, the universe may still contain:

- White dwarfs.
- [[sao-neutron|Neutron stars]].
- Cold planets.
- Asteroids.
- Black holes.

If protons decay, the ordinary matter remaining in many of these structures will also keep changing over extremely long spans of time.

The universe will hold fewer and fewer complex structures.

## A universe ever darker and more dilute

In the very far future, if protons decay and the universe keeps expanding, most of the structures familiar today will be gone.

In the end, there may be only:

- Light particles.
- Neutrinos.
- [[photon|Photons]] of ever lower energy.
- Black holes that survive for extremely long times before gradually losing their energy through Hawking radiation.

The universe would then be far colder, darker and more dilute than it is now.

## But this scenario may not happen

This is the most important point.

Proton decay is currently still **a physical hypothesis**, not a confirmed phenomenon.

So far:

- No proton has been confirmed to have decayed.
- Experiments only set ever larger lower limits on its lifetime.
- Some GUT models predict proton decay.
- Other models allow the proton to last much longer or possibly be stable.

Detecting even a single reliable proton-decay event would be one of the most important discoveries in modern physics, because it would provide direct evidence for physics beyond the Standard Model.

## Conclusion

⚛️ The proton is tiny, but its stability is one of the reasons matter can remain stable for billions of years.

If the proton really is immortal, atoms can go on existing into the extremely distant future.

If the proton can decay, ordinary matter also has an enormous "expiry date", and after an almost unimaginable length of time, the last planets, stellar remnants and atoms will gradually be transformed into light particles and radiation.

Until a proton is actually observed to decay, the question remains open:

> **Can matter exist forever, or will even the proton one day come to an end?**
