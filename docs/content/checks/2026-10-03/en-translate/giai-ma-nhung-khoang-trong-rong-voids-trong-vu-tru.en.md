## 🌌 What is a void?

Voids are enormous regions of the cosmic web with an unusually low density of galaxies.

While galaxies gather into:

- Galaxy groups
- Galaxy clusters
- Galaxy superclusters
- Cosmic filaments

between them lie stretches of space that are almost completely empty.

In other words:

> If galaxies are the "cities" of the universe, voids are the vast "deserts" that lie between them.

## 🌠 How empty is the universe?

Intergalactic space is one of the most perfect natural vacuums known.

### Extremely low matter density

On average, each cubic metre of space holds only a few [[proton|protons]] or atoms.

That figure is far lower than any vacuum humans have ever produced in a laboratory.

### Matter is unevenly distributed

The density of matter in the universe varies enormously:

| Region | Matter density |
|----------|----------------|
| Inside a star | Extremely high |
| Interstellar medium | Hundreds of thousands to millions of particles/m³ |
| Solar wind near Earth | A few million particles/m³ |
| Intergalactic space | Only a few particles/m³ |
| Inside a void | Lower still |

The emptiest regions of the universe have densities that come close to zero.

---

## ⏳ Why does the universe have such huge "holes"?

After the Big Bang, matter was distributed fairly evenly.

Over **13.8 billion years**, however, gravity completely reshaped the structure of the universe.

### Step 1: Gravity pulls matter together

Regions slightly denser than average attract more matter.

More and more matter flows into these regions and forms:

- Stars
- Galaxies
- Galaxy clusters

### Step 2: The surrounding regions are drained of matter

As matter keeps flowing into these "gravitational hot spots", the neighbouring regions become ever poorer in matter.

Over time, these sparse regions grow into enormous voids.

### Step 3: A cosmic spider web forms

The result is that today's universe resembles a giant spider web:

- The strands are where galaxies gather.
- The knots are galaxy clusters.
- The empty spaces in between are the voids.

This structure is called:

> **The Cosmic Web**

## 👁️ The Boötes Void: "The Great Nothing"

One of the best-known voids is:

**The Boötes Void**

also known as:

> **The Great Nothing**  
> **The Great Void**

### An astonishing size

The Boötes Void is roughly:

> **330 million [[nam-anh-sang|light-years]]** across

For comparison:

- The diameter of the Milky Way: about 105,000 light-years.
- The Boötes Void is more than 3,000 times larger than the Milky Way.

### How empty is it?

A region that size ought to contain thousands of galaxies.

Yet astronomers have found only a few dozen galaxies scattered inside it.

It is one of the regions with the lowest galaxy density ever observed.

### What if you stood at the centre of the Boötes Void?

Suppose you could stand in the middle of this void:

- No nearby stars.
- No bright band of the Milky Way.
- No prominent galaxies in the sky.

The space around you would be darker than any sky a human has ever seen.

## 🌡️ The temperature inside voids

Although they are almost empty, voids are not as cold as absolute zero.

Their background temperature is about:

> **2.725 kelvin**

equivalent to:

> **−270.4°C**

This heat comes from:

### The cosmic microwave background (CMB)

This is light left over from the earliest era of the universe.

It began as extremely hot radiation.

After more than 13.8 billion years of expansion:

- Its wavelength has been stretched.
- Its energy has dropped.
- It has become very cold microwave radiation.

The CMB is present throughout the universe and sets a shared "background temperature" even for the deepest voids.

Without the CMB:

> The temperature of voids would approach 0 kelvin.

---

## ❓ Are voids really "nothing"?

No.

According to modern physics, a vacuum is not the same as nothingness.

Even the emptiest regions of the universe still contain:

- A tiny amount of atoms and protons.
- Weak magnetic and electric fields.
- High-energy cosmic rays.
- The cosmic microwave background.
- Quantum fluctuations of the vacuum.

So:

> "Completely empty space" hardly exists in nature.

---

## 📌 Conclusion

🌌 **Cosmic voids** are the largest and most mysterious structures in the universe. They are not real "holes" but regions that gravity has drained of matter over billions of years of cosmic evolution.

Although almost empty, voids still contain radiation, physical fields and quantum effects. They help astronomers better understand how matter is distributed in the universe and how the vast cosmic web formed from tiny fluctuations after the Big Bang.

Put paradoxically:

> It is the regions with "almost nothing" in them that take up most of the space in the entire universe.
