## What is a runner's high?

A runner's high is a state that can appear during or after endurance exercise, with signs such as:

- A pleasant or euphoric feeling.
- Reduced anxiety.
- Reduced perception of pain.
- A sense of calm.
- Sometimes a sense that movement has become easier.

However, not every run produces a runner's high, and not everyone experiences it the same way.

## Endocannabinoids: "cannabinoids" the body makes itself

The human body has an **[[he-endocannabinoid|endocannabinoid system]] (ECS)**, made up of signalling molecules and receptors spread throughout the brain and many other tissues.

One well-known endocannabinoid is **anandamide**.

The name's resemblance to cannabis is no coincidence. The THC in cannabis acts on some of the receptors of this very system, whereas endocannabinoids are molecules the body produces itself.

After acute endurance exercise, many human studies have recorded rises in endocannabinoid levels. A 2022 systematic review with meta-analysis found that about **74%** of the samples in which anandamide was measured showed an increase after acute exercise. 

## So what about endorphins?

Endorphins are endogenous opioid peptides with roles in:

- Regulating pain.
- Responding to stress.
- Some of the body's responses to exercise.

However, the old hypothesis that endorphins in the blood directly produce the whole runner's high no longer stands alone: running raises blood levels of both β-endorphin and anandamide, and experiments in mice show that it is the cannabinoid receptors that mediate the anxiety-reducing and pain-reducing effects after running. 

Human experiments have also shown that the core features of a runner's high can still appear when opioid receptors are blocked, while evidence increasingly supports the role of the endocannabinoid system.

But that does not mean:

**Endorphins have no effect.**

The more accurate current understanding is that a runner's high may arise from the coordination of several systems, in which endocannabinoids play a prominent role, while endorphins, dopamine, serotonin and many other neuromodulators may also be involved. 

## How do endocannabinoids act?

Activation of the endocannabinoid system after exercise has been studied in relation to:

### Reduced perception of pain

A runner's high is often accompanied by **hypoalgesia**, that is, reduced sensitivity to pain after exercise.

### Reduced anxiety

Endocannabinoids are linked to the neural networks that regulate stress and anxiety. The rise in endocannabinoids after exercise correlates with some of the relaxing features of a runner's high.

### A pleasant feeling

A runner's high may include euphoria, calm and sometimes a state close to "flow". Modern reviews suggest this is the result of several neuromodulator systems working together, not the effect of a single "happiness hormone". 

## How long do you have to run to get a runner's high?

There is no formula such as:

**Run 30 minutes = runner's high**

or:

**70% heart rate = endocannabinoids switch on**

Research shows that endurance exercise at **moderate intensity** is usually favourable for raising endocannabinoids, but the response varies considerably between individuals and types of exercise. 

So a runner's high is not a reward guaranteed to appear once you have run enough kilometres.

One person may run for a very long time without feeling this state, while another experiences it quite clearly.

## Should you run very hard to "chase" a runner's high?

No.

A runner's high is not a measure of the quality of a workout.

Running too fast or too long just to seek euphoria can increase the load on the body without guaranteeing that the state will appear. Current evidence also does not identify a single duration or intensity threshold that guarantees a runner's high. 

The more sensible goals of running remain:

- Building fitness.
- Improving cardiovascular health.
- Supporting mental health.
- Building a long-term exercise habit.

## Conclusion

🏃 **The runner's high is a real phenomenon, but it is more complex than the story that "running makes the brain release endorphins".**

Modern research places the **endocannabinoid system** at the centre of the explanation, especially for the euphoria, reduced anxiety and reduced pain perception. However, the evidence in humans does not yet allow the whole phenomenon to be attributed to a single substance.

The best current understanding is:

**A runner's high is a "chemical symphony" of the brain and body, in which endocannabinoids may be among the most important conductors.**
