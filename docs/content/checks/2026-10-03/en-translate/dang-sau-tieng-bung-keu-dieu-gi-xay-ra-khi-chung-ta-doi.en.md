## The brain and the gut create hunger together

Hunger is regulated by many signals sent between the digestive tract and the brain.

One of the best-known hormones is **ghrelin**, produced mainly in the stomach. Ghrelin levels usually rise before meals and help stimulate the desire to eat. Besides ghrelin, hormones such as motilin, GLP-1 and CCK also take part in the system that controls hunger and fullness. 

**Leptin**, on the other hand, is secreted mainly by fat tissue and gives the brain information about the body's long-term energy stores.

So hunger is not switched on or off by a single hormone.

## Why does your stomach growl when you're hungry?

The scientific name for stomach growling is **borborygmi**.

When you have not eaten for a while, the stomach and small intestine go through cycles of contractions called the:

**Migrating Motor Complex (MMC)**

The MMC operates in the period between meals and is interrupted when we eat. Its strong waves of contraction help push whatever is left in the digestive tract further down. 

When the muscles contract, they push:

- Gas.
- Digestive fluids.
- A small amount of leftover material.

through the digestive tract.

It is this movement that creates the **"rumbling"** sound we hear.

Your stomach can still make sounds after eating, but when the stomach is relatively empty, the sounds are usually easier to notice.

## The MMC is like the gut's "cleaning mode"

The MMC is sometimes called the **"interdigestive housekeeper"**, a clean-up mechanism that runs between meals.

It helps move indigestible material along and is involved in controlling the distribution of bacteria in the small intestine. 

Notably, the MMC's strong contractions are also linked to the sensation of hunger. The hormone **motilin** is considered one of the key factors that trigger these contractions in humans. 

So:

**A growling stomach does not necessarily mean the body is "running out of energy".**

It may simply be that the digestive system is entering a cycle of movement between meals.

## Why does hunger sometimes make us irritable?

English has a playful word, **hangry**, a blend of *hungry* and *angry*.

This feeling may be linked to several factors:

- The discomfort of hunger.
- Reduced concentration.
- Expectations about mealtimes.
- Changes in metabolic and hormonal signals.
- Stress and pre-existing state of mind.

A healthy body has many mechanisms that keep blood glucose relatively stable between meals, so it should not be assumed that being hungry means the brain is in a state of "serious sugar shortage".

## Does skipping meals let acid "eat away" the stomach?

It is not that simple.

The stomach is built to hold a very strongly acidic environment. The stomach wall is protected by:

- A layer of mucus.
- Bicarbonate.
- Tightly joined epithelial cells.
- Mechanisms that renew the lining.

So **an empty stomach does not mean the acid will start digesting the stomach wall**.

Peptic ulcers are usually linked to more important causes such as *Helicobacter pylori* infection or the use of certain anti-inflammatory drugs.

Some people with reflux disease or digestive disorders may feel discomfort when they go hungry for a long time, but that is different from claiming that skipping meals by itself causes ulcers.

## Do you need to eat every time your stomach growls?

Not necessarily.

Stomach growling can happen even when the body still has plenty of stored energy.

If you are genuinely hungry and it is mealtime, a balanced meal with:

- [[protein|Protein]].
- Fibre.
- Vegetables.
- Suitable fats.
- Less refined carbohydrates.

usually keeps you full for longer than foods high in rapidly absorbed sugar.

Drinking water also makes sense if you are thirsty, but water should not be seen as a way to "trick the brain" in place of food when the body really needs a meal.

## When is hunger pain no longer normal?

Simple stomach growling is very common.

But if the feeling of "hunger" regularly comes with:

- Severe abdominal pain.
- Persistent burning.
- Vomiting.
- Black or bloody stools.
- Unintentional weight loss.
- Pain that wakes you at night.

then it should not all be put down to "an empty stomach"; it needs medical assessment.

## Conclusion

🍽️ A growling stomach is not simply an alarm bell saying the stomach has run out of food.

When we go without food, hormones and the nervous system control cycles of contraction in the stomach and small intestine. Gas and fluid pushed through the digestive tract create the familiar sounds, while signals such as ghrelin and motilin contribute to the desire to eat. 

In other words, **hunger is a conversation between the gut, hormones and the brain**, and stomach growling is just one of the sounds coming out of that complex conversation.
