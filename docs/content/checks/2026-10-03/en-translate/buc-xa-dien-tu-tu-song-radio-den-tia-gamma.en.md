## What is electromagnetic radiation?

Electromagnetic radiation is energy that travels through space in the form of changing electric and magnetic fields.

In a vacuum, all types of electromagnetic radiation travel at the speed:

**c = 299,792,458 m/s**

or roughly:

**300,000 km/s**

This holds for [[anh-sang-kha-kien|visible light]], radio waves, X-rays and gamma rays alike.

## The electromagnetic spectrum

All types of electromagnetic radiation together make up the **electromagnetic spectrum**.

In order from long wavelength and low frequency to short wavelength and high frequency:

### Radio waves

They have long wavelengths and low [[photon]] energy.

Uses:

- Radio broadcasting.
- Television.
- Radio communication.

### Microwaves

Used in:

- Wi-Fi.
- Radar.
- Satellite communication.
- Microwave ovens.

### Infrared

Objects at ordinary temperatures emit a lot of infrared radiation.

Uses:

- Thermal cameras.
- Remote controls.
- Infrared telescopes.

### Visible light

This is the very small part of the electromagnetic spectrum that the human eye can see:

**Red → Orange → Yellow → Green → Blue → Violet**

### Ultraviolet

Ultraviolet (UV) radiation has higher energy than visible light.

The Sun is an important source of UV. Some UV takes part in the synthesis of vitamin D, but excessive exposure can damage the skin and eyes.

### X-rays

X-rays have high energy and can pass through many kinds of material.

They are used in:

- X-ray imaging.
- CT scans.
- Materials testing.
- High-energy astronomy.

### Gamma rays

Gamma rays sit in the highest-energy region of the electromagnetic spectrum.

They can be produced in:

- Nuclear reactions.
- Radioactive decay.
- Extremely high-energy astronomical phenomena.

## How are wavelength, frequency and energy related?

Wavelength and frequency are inversely related:

**The longer the wavelength → the lower the frequency**

**The shorter the wavelength → the higher the frequency**

Photon energy, in turn, increases with frequency.

So:

**Radio waves → long wavelength, low energy**

**Gamma rays → short wavelength, high energy**

This is why X-rays and gamma rays can ionise matter, whereas ordinary radio waves do not have enough photon energy to do so.

## Why do our eyes see only part of the electromagnetic spectrum?

The human eye is sensitive only to a very narrow range of wavelengths called **visible light**.

But all around us there are also:

- Radio waves.
- Microwaves.
- Infrared.
- Ultraviolet.
- X-rays.
- Gamma rays.

We cannot see them with our eyes, but we can detect them with specialised instruments.

## How important is electromagnetic radiation?

Electromagnetic radiation underpins a great many modern technologies:

- Wi-Fi and mobile networks.
- GPS and satellites.
- Thermal cameras.
- Remote controls.
- X-ray imaging and CT.
- Telescopes.
- Solar cells.

In astronomy, observing different regions of the electromagnetic spectrum also lets us see completely different aspects of the same object.

🌈 The light the human eye can see is only a small slice of a vast "ocean" of radiation. Radio, Wi-Fi, infrared, light, X-rays and gamma rays are not separate phenomena; they are all different forms of the same electromagnetic radiation.
