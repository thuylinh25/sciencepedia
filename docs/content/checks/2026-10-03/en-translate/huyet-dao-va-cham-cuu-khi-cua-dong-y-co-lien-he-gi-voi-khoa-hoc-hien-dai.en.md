## 🧭 What are acupoints in traditional Eastern medicine?

According to traditional Eastern medicine:

- **Qi** is the form of energy that sustains life.
- **Meridians** are the channels through which Qi flows.
- **Acupoints** are the points where Qi flow meets or is regulated.

When Qi flows freely:

- The body is healthy.
- The organs work in balance.

When Qi is blocked or disordered:

- Pain appears.
- Bodily functions decline.
- Illness arises.

In this system, acupuncture is seen as a way to adjust and restore balance.

---

## 🔬 What has modern science found?

To date, anatomists have not found a distinct structure corresponding to the "meridians".

There are no:

- Qi-carrying ducts.
- Special energy channels.
- New anatomical organ systems.

Interestingly, however, many traditional acupoints lie close to:

- Nerve bundles.
- Muscle pain trigger points.
- Distinctive areas of connective tissue.
- Places where blood vessels and sensory receptors are concentrated.

This suggests that:

> Acupoints may not be "energy channels" in a physical sense, but many acupoint locations really are biologically sensitive points in the body.

---

## 🧠 How does acupuncture act on the nervous system?

When an acupuncture needle is inserted into the skin:

- Nerve receptors are stimulated.
- Electrical signals travel to the spinal cord.
- The brain receives the information and responds.

This mechanism has been documented in many neuroscience studies.

### Natural pain relief

Stimulation from the needle may prompt the brain to release:

- Endorphins.
- Enkephalins.

These are the body's own endogenous painkillers.

The result:

- The sensation of pain decreases.
- Some patients feel more relaxed.

### Regulating nervous activity

Acupuncture has also been studied in relation to:

- Serotonin.
- Dopamine.
- The autonomic nervous system.

These mechanisms may help explain the pain-relieving effect or symptom improvement seen in some people.

## 🩸 Why does the needled area often feel warm?

When a needle acts on tissue:

The body responds by:

- Increasing local blood flow.
- Dilating the capillaries.
- Mobilising tissue-repair mechanisms.

In the needled area:

- The skin may become redder.
- The temperature rises slightly.
- Circulation is temporarily improved.

This leads many people to connect it with the concept of:

> "Unblocking Qi and blood"

in traditional Eastern medicine.

Although the descriptive language differs, both approaches are talking about physiological changes taking place in the body.

---

## 🤔 Is acupuncture just a placebo effect?

This is the most hotly debated question.

Current research shows:

### Where the evidence is relatively good

Acupuncture may help with:

- Reducing chronic low back pain.
- Reducing neck pain.
- Tension headaches.
- Some musculoskeletal pain conditions.

### What remains controversial

For many other conditions:

- The mechanism is unclear.
- Study results are inconsistent.
- The [[gia-duoc|placebo effect]] may play part of the role.

So the scientific community generally does not regard acupuncture as a "miracle", but as a supportive method that may be useful in certain cases.

---

## 🌉 Where Eastern medicine and science meet

Although they use two completely different languages, they sometimes describe the same biological phenomenon.

| Traditional Eastern medicine | Modern science |
|---------|------------------|
| Qi | Physiological, neural and metabolic activity |
| Meridians | No clearly corresponding anatomical structure yet |
| Acupoints | Areas of neural, muscular or connective-tissue sensitivity |
| Unblocking Qi and blood | Nerve stimulation and increased blood circulation |
| Bodily balance | Regulation of neural and biological responses |

This does not mean the two systems are the same, but there are notable points of overlap.

---

## 📌 Conclusion

Acupoints are not "energy valves" proven by anatomy, and modern science has not found a physical meridian system in the body.

However:

✅ Many acupoints lie at locations of genuine neural and biological significance.

✅ Acupuncture can stimulate the nervous system, increase local circulation and help relieve pain.

✅ Some effects of acupuncture have been studied and documented in modern medicine.

So from the standpoint of today's science, acupuncture is not regarded as magic or mysterious energy, but can be understood as a method that stimulates the body's self-regulating mechanisms through the nervous system, circulation and natural biological responses.
