## 🦠 The simple world of organisms without self-awareness

A bacterium does not know that it exists.

A blade of grass does not wonder about the future.

A cell does not worry about death.

They simply respond to their environment according to simple biological rules:

- Find a source of energy.
- Grow.
- Reproduce.
- Adapt.

They have no need to know:

- Where they came from.
- What life means.
- What happens after death.

From one point of view, that is a kind of freedom humans have lost.

## 🧠 The moment the brain began to observe itself

For most of the history of life, organisms simply existed.

But somewhere along the path of [[tien-hoa|evolution]], the brain reached a new level of complexity:

> It not only processed the outside world but could also think about itself.

Humans do not just know.

Humans know that they know.

This is what scientists call:

> **Self-awareness**

From that moment, a whole series of questions appeared:

- Who am I?
- Why do I exist?
- What kind of person will I become?
- What happens when I die?

Those questions are the foundation of philosophy, religion, art and science.

But they are also the source of a great deal of mental suffering.

---

## ⏳ The price of knowing about the future

A deer runs from a lion.

Once it escapes, it goes back to grazing.

Humans are different.

We can sit in the safety of our own room and still worry about:

- Next week's meeting.
- Next year's finances.
- Growing old.
- Death in the distant future.

The human brain evolved to predict.

The ability to predict helped our ancestors survive.

But the downside is that:

> We can suffer over things that have never happened.

One study of people with generalised anxiety disorder recorded their daily worries and then tracked the outcomes for a month: more than 91% of the things they worried about did not happen — and yet the brain still spent energy preparing for them.

---

## 🌌 Existential loneliness

No other organism on Earth is known to be able to recognise the finiteness of life as clearly as humans do.

We know that:

- We were born.
- We are growing old.
- One day we will die.

That is a very special kind of awareness.

The psychologist Ernest Becker argued that much of human civilisation is an effort to come to terms with this fact.

From the Egyptian pyramids and epic poems to the works of modern science, humans have always sought to leave a trace that outlasts the limits of a single short life.

---

## ❤️ But consciousness also creates the most beautiful things

If consciousness brought only suffering, perhaps evolution would not have favoured it so much.

That same capacity for self-awareness also lets humans experience things that a bacterium or a blade of grass could never know:

- Love.
- Friendship.
- Music.
- Art.
- Curiosity.
- Compassion.

Only a brain that can think about death can truly cherish life.

Only a creature that realises how short time is can deeply feel the value of each moment.

---

## 🌠 Is consciousness a mistake of evolution?

We sometimes see anxiety, melancholy or existential crisis as flaws of the brain.

But from an evolutionary point of view, they may simply be unavoidable side effects of an extremely complex system.

Just as:

- The ability to remember comes with painful memories.
- The ability to love comes with loss.
- The ability to imagine comes with anxiety.

The dark and bright sides of consciousness may be two sides of the same coin.

You cannot keep one side and completely get rid of the other.

---

## 📌 Conclusion

Consciousness may be evolution's most extraordinary achievement, but it is not a free gift.

🌱 It gives us the ability to understand the universe.

💭 It gives us the ability to reflect on ourselves.

❤️ It gives us the ability to love, to create and to search for meaning.

But at the same time:

🌑 It also brings anxiety, loneliness and awareness of the finiteness of human life.

Perhaps that is why being human is always an experience both beautiful and difficult. We cannot go back to the unconscious state of simpler organisms. All we can do is learn to live with this special gift.

> Consciousness is not a burden set against life. Consciousness is the price the universe has to pay to be able, for once, to look back and understand itself.
