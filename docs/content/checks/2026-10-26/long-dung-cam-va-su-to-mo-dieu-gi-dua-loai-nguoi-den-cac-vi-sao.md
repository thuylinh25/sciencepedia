### D3 — điền trường contentEn

```markdown
## Curiosity is the engine of civilisation

Ever since they appeared on Earth, humans have been driven by one simple question:

> "What lies on the other side?"

That question led our ancestors to:

- Leave Africa.
- Cross the oceans.
- Explore the continents.
- Reach the North and South Poles.
- Turn their gaze to the sky.

Every great step forward for civilisation began with curiosity.

Today, telescopes such as Hubble and [James Webb](/articles/kinh-james-webb-nhin-nguoc-ve-thuo-vu-tru-so-sinh) carry on what the explorers of old once did: looking further, towards places no one has ever set foot.

## The universe is not only our destination, but our origin

Modern science shows that many of the elements that make up the human body were forged inside stars. Carbon and oxygen formed through nuclear fusion in the cores of ancient [stars](/articles/ngoi-sao-cau-tao-va-vong-doi), while most of the calcium and iron came from exploding stars: the supernovae that end the lives of massive stars.

In other words:

- We are made of [the material of stars](/articles/ban-khong-chi-song-trong-vu-tru-ban-la-mot-phan-cua-no).
- Every atom in the body has a history stretching back billions of years.
- The origin of humankind is bound up with the history of the universe.

That is why exploring space is not only a journey outward, but also a journey to understand where we come from.

## Courage is the next step after curiosity

Curiosity lets us see a new horizon.

Courage lets us walk towards it.

The history of space exploration was built by people willing to face risk in order to push back the limits of human knowledge.

They include:

- Yuri Gagarin, who made humanity's first flight into space.
- Neil Armstrong, who set foot on the Moon.
- The astronauts who live for months aboard the International Space Station.
- The engineers who send [probes to planets billions of kilometres away](/articles/tuyet-ky-di-ke-hanh-tinh-cach-tau-vu-tru-bay-hang-ty-kilomet-ma-khong-ton-them-nhien-lieu).

These achievements did not come from recklessness, but from a determination to explore the unknown.

## Is space exploration a luxury?

People sometimes ask:

> Why explore the universe when Earth still has so many problems to solve?

Yet the pursuit of new frontiers has repeatedly produced technology that is useful right here on the ground. The clearest example is the CMOS image sensor: a team at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) developed it to shrink the cameras on spacecraft while keeping image quality good enough for science, and today that kind of sensor is in most mobile phone cameras. NASA has recorded thousands of "spin-off" technologies that came about in a similar way.

Many familiar services also depend directly on satellites in orbit. Weather forecasting is one example: the US National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) has used satellites to monitor the weather and produce forecasts for more than fifty years.

More importantly, when we look at Earth from space, we realise that every human being is living together on one small planet in a vast universe.

## What makes humans special

Humans are not the strongest.

Not the fastest.

Not the largest.

What sets us apart is the ability to imagine things that do not yet exist, and the determination to make them real.

We build telescopes to see further than our eyes.

We build rockets to travel further than our feet.

We build probes to reach worlds never known before.

Each of those steps began with a question, and the courage to pursue the answer.

## Conclusion

The universe may be so vast that a human being is no more than a tiny speck of dust. But that speck of dust has learned to ask where the stars come from, to [measure the age of the universe](/articles/big-bang-vu-tru-da-dien-ra-the-nao-trong-138-ti-nam), and to send machines far beyond the Solar System.

The capacity to be curious, to learn and to keep exploring is one of humanity's most remarkable qualities. Perhaps that is why the journey to the stars is not only a scientific goal, but also the most natural expression of human nature: always wanting to know what lies ahead.
```
