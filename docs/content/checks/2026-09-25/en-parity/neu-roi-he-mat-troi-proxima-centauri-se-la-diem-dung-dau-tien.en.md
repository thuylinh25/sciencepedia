## What is a red dwarf?

Red dwarfs are the most common type of star in the Milky Way galaxy, accounting for roughly **70% to 75% of all stars**.

Compared to the Sun, they:

- Are much smaller in mass.
- Have lower surface temperatures.
- Are much dimmer.
- Have extremely long lifespans, ranging from hundreds of billions to trillions of years.

## Could planets around Proxima Centauri harbor life?

Because red dwarfs emit little energy, the zone where liquid water could exist lies very close to the host star.

This creates two major challenges:

- Planets there are prone to being **[[khoa-thuy-trieu|tidally locked]]**, with one side always in daylight and the other always in night.
- Powerful flares from the star can strip away part of a planet's atmosphere.

Even so, **Proxima Centauri b** is still considered one of the most notable candidates in the search for life beyond the Solar System.

## How long would it take to reach Proxima Centauri?

The distance to Proxima Centauri is approximately **40 trillion km**.

The figures below are simple conversions: they assume a spacecraft keeps its current speed and flies in a straight line toward Proxima. They are not real trajectories; Voyager 1, for instance, is not headed that way.

- **Voyager 1** (about 17 km/s relative to the Sun): about 75,000 years.
- **New Horizons**: about 90,000 years.
- **Parker Solar Probe**: about 6,700 years, *but this is only a conversion from its record speed at closest approach to the Sun. The probe is in a closed orbit around the Sun and cannot leave the Solar System.*

## Future hope

The **Breakthrough Starshot** project proposes ultra-light probes on lightsails, pushed by a powerful ground-based laser array.

Its stated goal is speeds of up to about **15% of the speed of light**, which would let a probe **fly past** the Alpha Centauri system just over **20 years** after launch. It would be a flyby, not a stop.

## Proxima Centauri's planetary system

The planets reported so far are:

- **Proxima Centauri b**: a planet with a minimum mass of about one Earth mass, orbiting within the star's habitable zone. It may be rocky, but its size and composition have not been measured.
- **Proxima Centauri c**: a signal proposed in 2020 that has **not been confirmed**.
- **Proxima Centauri d**: a very light planet (minimum mass about 0.26 Earth masses) orbiting close to the star with a period of just 5.1 days, closer in than the inner edge of the habitable zone. Its equilibrium temperature may reach about 360 K, assuming a Bond albedo of 0.3.

⭐ If humans ever make a first interstellar journey, Proxima Centauri, the nearest star beyond the Sun, is the obvious first candidate destination.
