## Newton's three laws of motion

### First law: inertia

> An object stays at rest, or keeps moving in a straight line at constant speed, unless a net force acts on it.

This means no force is needed to keep something moving; a force is needed only to change its speed or its direction of motion.

### Second law: force and acceleration

Newton's best-known law:

> F = ma

- The larger the force, the faster the object speeds up.
- The larger the mass, the harder it is to change the object's motion.

Pushing a bicycle, for example, is easier than pushing a car.

### Third law: action and reaction

> For every force there is always a reaction force of equal magnitude in the opposite direction.

Examples:

- Your foot pushes the ground backwards; the ground pushes your body forwards.
- A rocket pushes hot gas out behind it; the hot gas pushes the rocket forwards.

## How did Newton explain Kepler?

Kepler found that the planets move around the Sun in elliptical orbits.

Newton went a step further by proposing that:

> Every object with mass attracts every other object through [[luc-hap-dan|gravity]].

By combining gravity with the laws of motion, Newton showed why the planets must move exactly as Kepler had observed.

- **Kepler described what happens.**
- **Newton explained why.**

## Do Newton's three laws still hold today?

Yes. They are still used to:

- Design bridges, roads and vehicles.
- Calculate the orbits of satellites and rockets.
- Simulate motion in engineering.

Under extreme conditions, however — at speeds close to the speed of light, or near a black hole — Newtonian physics has to give way to Einstein's theory of relativity.

Newton did not just explain the motion of an apple or of a planet. He found general laws that govern every object with mass in the universe, laying the foundations of modern physics.
