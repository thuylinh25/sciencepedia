## The four basic quantities of a wave

### Wavelength (λ)

The distance between two successive crests.

### Frequency (f)

The number of oscillations that take place in one second.

It is measured in **hertz (Hz)**.

### Period (T)

The time taken to complete one oscillation.

Formula:

> T = 1/f

### Amplitude

The greatest displacement of the wave from its equilibrium position.

The larger the amplitude, the more energy the wave carries.

## How wavelength and frequency are related

The speed at which a wave travels is given by:

> v = λf

where:

- v: wave speed
- λ: wavelength
- f: frequency

For light in a vacuum:

> c = λf

This is the basic relation for every periodic wave.

## Mechanical waves and electromagnetic waves

### Mechanical waves

Mechanical waves need a medium to travel through.

Examples:

- Sound
- Water waves
- Seismic waves

Sound cannot travel through a vacuum, because there is no medium to vibrate.

### Electromagnetic waves

[[buc-xa-dien-tu|Electromagnetic waves]] do not need a medium.

Examples:

- Light
- Radio waves
- X-rays
- Gamma rays

That is how sunlight can cross the emptiness of space to reach Earth.

## Transverse and longitudinal waves

### Transverse waves

The oscillation is perpendicular to the direction the wave travels.

Examples:

- Light
- Waves on a string

### Longitudinal waves

The oscillation is parallel to the direction the wave travels.

Examples:

- Sound waves in air
- P waves from an earthquake

## Seismic waves reveal the structure of the Earth

An earthquake produces two main kinds of body wave:

### P waves

- Travel through both solids and liquids.
- Are the first to arrive.

### S waves

- Travel only through solids.
- Cannot pass through liquids.

The fact that S waves cannot cross the outer core helped scientists establish that the Earth's outer core is liquid.

## What happens when a wave meets an obstacle?

### Reflection

The wave bounces back into the medium it came from.

### Refraction

The wave changes direction as it passes from one medium into another.

Because different wavelengths of light are slowed by different amounts, they bend at different angles — which is how a prism splits white light into colours, and part of how a rainbow forms.

### Diffraction

The wave bends and spreads out as it passes through a narrow gap or around an obstacle.

From sound and light to earthquakes, periodic waves are all described by the same four simple quantities: wavelength, frequency, period and amplitude. These quantities are what let us understand how energy travels through nature and across the universe.
