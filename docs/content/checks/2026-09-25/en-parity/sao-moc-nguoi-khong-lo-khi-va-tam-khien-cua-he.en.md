## No surface to stand on

Jupiter is made mostly of hydrogen and helium. Descending from the cloud tops, the pressure rises until hydrogen turns into a liquid metallic state — thought to conduct electricity and to generate the strongest magnetic field of any planet. Two quantities need to be kept apart: Jupiter's magnetic MOMENT is about 20,000 times Earth's, while the STRENGTH of its magnetic field at the surface is only about 16–54 times Earth's. Saying "a magnetic field 20,000 times stronger" leaves a mental model that is wrong by a factor of about a thousand.

There is no clear boundary between "atmosphere" and "surface". The Galileo probe, released into the atmosphere in 1995, stopped working after 58 minutes, at a depth of about 200 km.

## Great Red Spot

An anticyclone wider than Earth, tracked continuously since about 1831. Cassini described a "Permanent Spot" at the same latitude in 1665–1713, but that structure disappeared, and no chain of observations connects it to today's spot. Sánchez-Lavega and colleagues (2024) concluded it is "highly unlikely" that the current spot is that same Permanent Spot — the conclusion of one specific study, not a settled consensus. Winds in the outermost ring of the vortex exceed 640 km/h (Hubble measurements, 2009–2020). Notably, the spot is shrinking and becoming rounder at the same time: from 39,000 km along its longest axis, measured in 1879, to about 14,000 km according to measurements published in 2024.

## A moon system like a miniature planetary system

The four Galilean moons were discovered by Galileo Galilei in 1610 — an observation that showed clearly that not every body circles the Earth:

- **Io** — the most volcanically active body in the Solar System
- **Europa** — much evidence points to a salty ocean beneath its icy shell, and it is considered one of the most promising places to search for life
- **Ganymede** — the largest moon in the system, larger even than Mercury, and the only moon known to have its own magnetic field
- **Callisto** — an ancient surface, densely covered with impact craters

## Role in the system

Jupiter's [[luc-hap-dan|gravity]] shapes the asteroid belt and affects the orbits of [comets](/articles/sao-choi-nguon-goc-cau-tao-va-so-phan). Its role as "Earth's protective shield" is a long-standing hypothesis that is still debated. Horner and Jones (2008) simulated asteroid populations and found the picture "far less clear cut": rather than only blocking, Jupiter may "enhance the impact rate of asteroids on the Earth significantly". It both deflects some objects and pushes others inward.
