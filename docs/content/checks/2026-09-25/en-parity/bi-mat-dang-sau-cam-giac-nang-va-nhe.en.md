## 1. Mass: a measure of an object's "sluggishness"

Mass is first of all a measure of **[[quan-tinh|inertia]]**: an object's resistance to a change in its state of motion.

This is described by Newton's second law:

**F = m × a**

Where:

- **F**: applied force (N)
- **m**: mass (kg)
- **a**: [[gia-toc|acceleration]] (m/s²)

### What it means in practice

For the same applied force:

- An object with a small mass speeds up more quickly.
- An object with a large mass speeds up more slowly.

That is why:

- Pushing a bicycle is fairly easy.
- Pushing a car is much harder.

A car is not more "sluggish" because the Earth pulls on it harder, but because it has a **larger inertial mass**.

## 2. Weight: the force of gravity on an object

Unlike mass, **weight** is a force.

Weight is given by:

**W = m × g**

Where:

- **W**: weight (N)
- **m**: mass (kg)
- **g**: acceleration due to gravity (m/s²)

### The key point

Mass stays practically the same when you change location.

But **weight changes with the gravity where you stand**.

For example:

- On Earth: g ≈ 9.81 m/s²
- On the Moon: g ≈ 1.62 m/s²

This means:

> A person with a mass of 60 kg on Earth still has a mass of 60 kg on the Moon.

But their weight there is only about **1/6** as much.

That is why astronauts can bound so high across the surface of the Moon.

## 3. Why are astronauts on the ISS "weightless"?

This is one of the most common misconceptions about space.

Many people believe there is no gravity on the International Space Station (ISS).

In fact:

> At the altitude of the ISS, Earth's gravity is still about 90% as strong as at the surface.

So why do the astronauts float?

### It is continuous free fall

The ISS moves around the Earth at about 7.7 km/s.

It is constantly falling towards the Earth under gravity, but at the same time it is moving sideways fast enough to keep "missing" the curved surface of the Earth.

As a result:

- The space station is falling.
- The astronauts are falling.
- Everything inside is falling too.

All of it with the same acceleration.

### No more support from the floor

When you stand on the ground, you feel your weight because the floor pushes back up on your feet.

On the ISS:

- The station's floor is falling.
- Your body is falling.

So that supporting force does not exist.

You are still acted on by gravity, but you no longer feel "pressed down".

This is called:

> **Microgravity**

not a complete absence of gravity.

## 4. A puzzle of physics: two kinds of mass

Modern physics distinguishes two kinds of mass:

### Inertial mass

Describes an object's resistance to being accelerated when a force acts on it.

It appears in:

**F = m × a**

### Gravitational mass

Describes how strongly an object:

- Attracts other objects.
- Is attracted by other objects.

It appears in the equations of gravitation.

## The strange part

These two quantities are different in nature:

- One is about motion.
- The other is about gravitation.

Yet the most precise experiments ever carried out have found no difference between them:

> Inertial mass = Gravitational mass

to within extremely tight limits.

It was this equivalence that led Einstein to the **Equivalence Principle**, the foundation of General Relativity.

However:

> Why two seemingly unrelated quantities should always be equal remains one of the deepest questions in modern physics.

## A striking example: equipment weighing several tonnes in space

Suppose an astronaut on the ISS pushes a piece of equipment with a mass of a few tonnes.

A push can set it drifting, because there is almost no friction and it has no weight that anyone can feel — but, having a large mass, it gains speed only slowly.

But if the equipment drifts into the astronaut:

- Its mass is still there.
- Its inertia is still there.

An object of several tonnes can still produce a very large impact force.

That is why:

> "Weightless" does not mean "massless".

## Conclusion

Mass, weight and inertia are three related but different concepts:

- **Mass** measures the amount of matter in an object and its inertia.
- **Inertia** is the tendency to resist changes in motion.
- **Weight** is the force of gravity acting on an object.

Understanding the difference explains many seemingly paradoxical phenomena, from jumping high on the Moon to astronauts floating inside the ISS. An object can have almost no weight that you can feel, yet its mass and inertia remain fully intact.
