## Why aren't we thrown off the Earth?

### Gravity always prevails

Everything on Earth is pulled toward the center of the planet by gravity. Earth's rotation does produce an outward centrifugal effect, but it is very small compared to gravity.

Right at the equator, where the rotational speed is highest, this effect offsets only about **0.35%** of gravity. That is far too little to fling people, or anything else, off the surface of the planet.

### We are moving with the Earth

Not only the ground but also the atmosphere, the oceans and every object on Earth essentially rotate along with the planet. We have been moving this way since birth, so we do not feel it.

The same thing happens on an airliner in steady cruise. Even though the plane is flying at hundreds of km/h, passengers feel almost stationary as long as there is no turbulence and no sudden change in speed.

### What the body can actually sense

The nervous system and the vestibular system in the inner ear respond to **[[gia-toc|acceleration]]**, that is, a change in speed or direction of motion, as well as to the pull of gravity itself.

Strictly speaking, anyone standing on the rotating Earth is always accelerating, because moving in a circle means constantly changing direction. But that acceleration is tiny (it is the same 0.35% of gravity mentioned above), and it does not change: Earth turns at an almost perfectly steady rate. A small, constant acceleration simply blends into our sense of weight and of which way is "down", so there is nothing new for our senses to register.

## Conclusion

🌍 We do not feel Earth's rotation because the acceleration it gives us is very small and never changes, not because we are not accelerating at all. Earth turns at an almost constant rate, and the atmosphere and everything on the surface move along with it. The centrifugal effect of that motion is real, but at the equator it offsets only about **0.35%** of gravity, and because it is constant it is indistinguishable from a very slightly weaker gravity.
